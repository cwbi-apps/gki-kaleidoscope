Assess sites

Kaleidoscope

An AI-driven virtual assistant for asking questions of published data in plain language, with answers on a map. USACE staff use it to assess sites for flood risk reduction. Kaleidoscope continues to evolve as more data is integrated and new use cases are added.

  • Ask what is in the floodplain, what a levee protects, or what lies within a project area
  • Get answers grounded in authoritative, linked data rather than guesswork
  • Trace each answer back to the datasets behind it

Integrate data to enable knowledge sharing and AI compatibility

A geospatial knowledge infrastructure (GKI) connects data from many sources through location and the relationships between real-world features, so knowledge, not just datasets, can be shared across organizations and used directly by AI. Geoprism Registry and Kaleidoscope are the tools within the USACE GKI for combining data, enhancing it for advanced analytics and integrating it with AI. USACE staff build and link the data in Geoprism Registry, then publish it to Kaleidoscope to assess sites for flood risk reduction.

How it works

  1. Integrate

    Combine disparate datasets from many agencies based on location.

  2. Enhance

    Validate the data, relate features to one another and record where each value came from.

  3. Publish

    Release versioned linked data to Kaleidoscope and other systems.

  4. Assess

    Ask questions in Kaleidoscope to assess sites for flood risk reduction.

Supporting USACE VAULTIS objectives

The GKI supports the VAULTIS goals of making data visible, accessible, understandable, linked, trustworthy and interoperable. Geoprism Registry publishes versioned data that staff and systems can find and use, defines what each feature and relationship means, links datasets through location, records where each value came from and uses open standards, including OGC standards where they apply, to exchange data with other systems.

The work also aligns with the interoperability objectives of the Army Unified Data Reference Architecture (UDRA) and the AI scalability objectives of America’s AI Action Plan.

Current focus: integrity, provenance and trust

Ongoing work improves usability and scalability and draws on the latest OGC Domain Working Group developments to establish Integrity, Provenance, and Trust (IPT), in collaboration with the Federal Geographic Data Committee (FGDC) and using the Internet of Water Coalition’s published linked data.

Developed by TerraFrame for the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE), with support from the Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC). Geoprism Registry is an open-source digital public good, originally developed by TerraFrame and managed independently of USACE, with significant contributions from USACE.
